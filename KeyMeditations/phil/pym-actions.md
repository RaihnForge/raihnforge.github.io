The sentiment is ancient, but the English paper trail runs through the 1600s: a parliamentary speech from the era of John Pym — "a word spoken in season is like an apple of gold... but a good deed is better" in spirit — and Gersham Bulkeley's 1692 tract Will and Doom, which states it nearly in the modern form. It survived four centuries because it's an information-theory claim, not a moral one: words are cheap to produce, so they carry little signal; action is costly, so it carries a lot. Talk is a bid; behavior is the payment. Its cousin on this deck is the fruits card — same test, agricultural metaphor.
