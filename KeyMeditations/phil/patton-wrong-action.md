Patton's actual line, from War As I Knew It, is "a good plan violently executed now is better than a perfect plan executed next week." The Keyes compression pushes it further — even the wrong action beats the perfect one that never ships — and that's defensible on Patton's own logic: in a moving situation, waiting is not neutral. The perfect plan is being drafted against a battlefield that no longer exists, while action generates the one thing planning can't: contact with reality, and information. The caveat lives in the deck's discipline column — this is a doctrine for reversible decisions and fluid situations, not for the handful of one-way doors.
