Nearly every tradition has a version of this rule, but most state it negatively — Hillel: "What is hateful to you, do not do to your fellow"; Confucius: "Do not impose on others what you do not desire." The Sermon on the Mount formulation is distinctive because it is positive and therefore active: not merely refrain from harm, but go find the good you'd want done and do it first. That flips the rule from a restraint into an assignment. It also quietly assumes the work of the Self-Command cards — you have to actually know how you'd want to be treated, honestly, before you can extend it outward.
