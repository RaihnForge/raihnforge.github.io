The original context is a warning about false prophets — people whose words are precisely engineered to pass inspection. So the test moves to something harder to fake: output over time. A tree cannot sustain fruit contrary to its nature; a person cannot sustain results contrary to their character. Note the patience built into the metaphor — fruit takes a season, so the verdict isn't available on day one, and one bad apple isn't the harvest. As an evaluation tool it's the ancestor of every "show me, don't tell me": trust trajectories and track records, discount manifestos. And it points back at yourself first — your own fruit is also on display.
