The words predate Socrates — γνῶθι σεαυτόν was carved at the entrance to Apollo's temple at Delphi, a warning to pilgrims about to ask a god for answers: audit the asker first. Socrates turned the inscription into a life's method, examining himself and everyone else in Athens until they killed him for it, insisting that the unexamined life is not worth living. Two thousand years on it remains step zero of this whole deck: the internal-state axiom assumes you can read your own gauges; the plank assumes you'll look for it; the middle path assumes you know which extreme is currently seducing you. Every other card is downstream of this one.
