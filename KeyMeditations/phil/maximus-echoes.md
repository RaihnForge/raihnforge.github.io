Maximus says this to steady his cavalry before the opening battle in Germania — a fictional general in a film about a real Stoic court. The irony is worth keeping: Marcus Aurelius, the emperor Maximus serves, spends much of the actual Meditations reminding himself that posthumous fame is worthless, that even the longest-remembered names dissolve. So why does the movie line still land? Because it isn't about fame. It's about consequence. Actions propagate through the people they touch, and those people act on others, far past your sight and your lifetime. You don't get to be remembered; you do get to be causal. Read that way, the Hollywood line and the Stoic emperor stop arguing with each other.
