The joke of Galaxy Quest is that a washed-up actor's hollow catchphrase gets taken literally by aliens who built a civilization on reruns — and the arc of the film is the phrase becoming true, because people kept acting as if it were. That's a real mechanic wearing a comedy costume: creeds work by repetition before belief. Its serious ancestor is Churchill at Harrow in 1941 — "never give in, never, never, never... except to convictions of honour and good sense" — and Churchill's forgotten exception matters: persistence is for worthy aims, not sunk costs. Keep the exception in the footnote and the war cry in your mouth.
