Peterson's rendering of the Beatitude turns on the Greek praus — the word translated "meek," which was also used for war-horses: immense power, trained to command. His paraphrase — those who have swords and know how to use them, but keep them sheathed, will inherit the world — is a contested translation but a very old ethic: harmlessness is only a virtue when harm was an option. A person with no capacity for force isn't peaceful, just safe. The sheathed sword requires two disciplines at once: the years of training that make the blade real, and the daily restraint that keeps it put away. Roosevelt's big stick is the statecraft edition.
