Roosevelt introduced it at the Minnesota State Fair in 1901, crediting a West African proverb: "Speak softly and carry a big stick; you will go far." As foreign policy it meant negotiating quietly while the fleet made the quiet credible. The personal version is the same mechanic: courtesy backed by capability. The soft walk without the stick is timidity that invites the test; the stick without the soft walk is a bully looking for one. The proverb only works with both halves installed — and the stick's whole purpose is to be visible enough that it never has to move. Peterson's sheathed sword is this idea wearing different clothes.
