The image is deliberately absurd — a man with a beam of lumber through his own eye performing eye surgery on his neighbor — and the absurdity is the argument. Judgment isn't forbidden here; it's sequenced. The passage ends "then you will see clearly to remove the speck," which means the goal is still clear sight and even correction — but self-audit is the prerequisite, not an optional virtue. The plank also explains why hypocrisy is uniquely blinding: your own faults don't just discredit your critique, they distort the very perception the critique depends on. Order of operations: instrument check before measurement.
